A LITTLE DEEPER / SCALE, REACHABILITY & OPEN QUESTIONS

01 / GOD’S NUMBER

“Shortest for every start” has a measurable limit.

God’s number is the greatest, over all legal positions, of the length of a shortest solution. For the 3×3×3 it is 20 moves in the half-turn metric (a face turn, including a half-turn, costs one). For variable-size n×n×n cubes, the diameter grows as Θ(n² / log n) under the model studied by Demaine et al.

Rokicki et al., The Diameter of the Rubik’s Cube Group Is Twenty (SIAM, 2013) ↗
02 / COMPLEXITY

NP-hardness depends on the exact puzzle definition.

A fixed 3×3×3 cube has a finite state space; “NP-hard” is not a useful blanket label for it. A known result proves finding an optimal solution NP-hard for an n×n×1 variant when some cubie positions and colors are ignored. That is not the same claim as NP-hardness for the ordinary, fully specified n×n×n cube.

Demaine et al., Algorithms for Solving Rubik’s Cubes ↗
03 / PERMUTATION PARITY

Not every sticker arrangement is reachable.

Legal turns preserve reachability invariants. On a 3×3×3, corner and edge permutation parity must agree; corner-twist and edge-flip totals are constrained too. Larger cubes have additional center and wing-piece constraints. “Parity” cases in big-cube solving are reachable states that look impossible after reduction—not permission for arbitrary permutations.

04 / WHAT COULD WE EXPLORE?

Good questions are still open at the practical scale.

How can stronger pattern databases be built within a fixed memory budget? Which move metric best reflects human or robot turns? Can we certify short solutions without storing enormous search frontiers? Comparing optimality, computation, and explainability is a rich project in its own right.

At cube scale, the trick isn’t checking every path—it’s choosing what the search can afford to remember.
